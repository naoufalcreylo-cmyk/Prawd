Most football shirts look outward, to whichever template the manufacturer is offering this season. Walthamstow F.C.'s 2023-24 home shirt looked closer to home.

The shirt came out of a collaboration between the club, the William Morris Gallery, the Walthamstow design collective Wood Street Walls and the sportswear brand Admiral. The gallery, which stands in the part of east London where Morris spent his youth, opened up its archive. The blue surface adapts "Yare", designed around 1892 by John Henry Dearle for Morris & Co. A pattern first printed by hand-block on cotton ended up running down a touchline in non-league football, sublimated into polyester.

According to the Victoria and Albert Museum, which acquired one for its collection, the shirts were released in July 2023 and sold out within days, with sales intended to help fund a women's team in Waltham Forest.

## Why it works

What's satisfying about the idea is that it's so specific. Football has seen plenty of decorative shirts in recent years, as digital printing has made elaborate all-over patterns cheap. Many are perfectly attractive and mean very little. This one has a reason to exist, and it could only belong to this club.

The pattern also suits the job more than you might expect. Morris & Co. designs were built to repeat across large areas while keeping their rhythm and legibility, and that's essentially what a shirt needs as well. At a distance it reads as a textured blue. Up close, it rewards you with leaves and stems.

## A lesson for smaller clubs

Non-league clubs rarely have the budget to compete with the top of the game, but it may be that they have the better brief. A small club has a real place to draw from: a local industry, a building, a piece of history that nobody else can claim. And a small club can afford to take a risk, because nobody's going to complain about a sponsor's sightlines.

Plenty of clubs at this level have taken on the idea in recent years, with shirts raising money for local causes or celebrating neighbourhood landmarks. The best of them show a simple thing, which big clubs with larger design departments often forget. The most interesting design reference is usually very close to home.
