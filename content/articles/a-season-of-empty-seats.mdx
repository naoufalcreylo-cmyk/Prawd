The photograph shows a row of plastic seats at The Dripping Pan, the home of Lewes FC in East Sussex. They were photographed on 30 August 2020, at a pre-season game between Lewes FC Women and London Bees, and many have been taped or marked to show that no one should sit in them. They're red and black, slightly faded, and they're the most ordinary objects imaginable. A few years on, the image already looks like something from another world.

## A strange kind of quiet

Anyone who watched football during the pandemic remembers how it sounded. Top-level matches went ahead behind closed doors, and suddenly you could hear what the players actually said to each other. Broadcasters piped in crowd noise as an option, an artificial atmosphere that fooled nobody and yet, for many, was better than the alternative.

Lower down the pyramid, the problem was more practical. Clubs at that level depend on gate money, bar takings and the people who come through the turnstile. Many spent 2020 working out how to safely let a small crowd in at all, with one-way routes, hand gel and, as here, tape on seats. Volunteers who usually sold programmes were now measuring distances.

## What the crosses show

The seats make a simple point. A football ground is designed around people who are close together, and the whole idea of the terrace or the stand is that you lose your personal space. Any club secretary knows that the crowd isn't simply watching the event. In important ways it's part of what the event is.

The experiment of playing without supporters was never really a fair test of anything. But it did settle an old argument. Whatever else was going on, football without a crowd turned out to be a different thing, and it wasn't one that many people wanted.

## Tape comes off

The markings at grounds like this were eventually peeled away. Crowds came back, people went back to standing too close, and complaining about it. The picture remains as a small, accidental document, and it's worth a look now and then, the next time you're at a game and someone's elbow is in your ribs.
