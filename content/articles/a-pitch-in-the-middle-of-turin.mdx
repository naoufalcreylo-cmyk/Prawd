The photograph has no known author and no exact date. The file's description says only that it shows Campo Juventus, commonly known as the Stadio di Corso Marsiglia after its location, and that the ground was home to Juventus between 1922 and 1939.

So the picture must come from within those years, or close to them. It has faded to the colour of weak tea. A walled rectangle with a marked pitch sits at the centre, with covered stands along its sides and a second, smaller field beyond. Streets press up to the outside. It looks more like a well-kept municipal facility than the home of one of the most famous clubs in the world.

## What an aerial view does

Aerial pictures of old grounds have a particular appeal. Photographs taken at pitch level tell you about the match. A view from above tells you about the relationship between a ground and its city: how far from the centre it sat, what surrounded it and how tightly it was fitted in.

They're also among the most useful records we have of grounds that no longer exist. Stands get rebuilt and sites get redeveloped, and the small details disappear. The people who remember them go too. But a single good photograph from the air fixes the whole arrangement in place.

## Starting small

It's easy to forget that every big club was once a modest local concern, playing in a walled field beside a road. Looking at this picture, you can't see any of what was to come. You just see a pitch in a city, carefully marked out and ready for the weekend.
