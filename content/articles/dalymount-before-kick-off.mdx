The picture at the top of this page, taken in 2012, shows one of the entrances to Dalymount Park, with a metal arch, red doors and a collection of signs put up over the years. Nobody would call it elegant. But there's no mistaking it, and no chance that it could be anywhere else.

Bohemians have played at Dalymount since the start of the twentieth century, and the entrance looks it, in the best sense. Layers of paint, signs and small repairs build up on a place like this. You can compare it with the street art nearby, which PRAWD looked at in [Who paints the pitch?](/articles/who-paints-the-pitch).

New stadiums tend to be planned so that every entrance looks like every other. Old ones have to be learned, which is part of their appeal. Regulars know exactly which door is theirs.

## The ground, waiting

Go through the door and you get the other picture.

<Figure id="dalymount-panorama" />

This panorama was taken in 2013, near the corner of two of the ground's stands, before Bohemian FC played Limerick. Most of the frame is grass and evening sky. Low stands run along the far side, with the buildings of Phibsborough on the north side of Dublin behind them.

Grounds spend more time like this than any other way. There's a particular quiet in the half hour before the crowd has fully arrived, when the pitch is empty and the stands are still mostly just shapes. It's easy to overlook, because nobody goes to a match to look at the pitch before anything has happened on it. But it's a fine time to be there.
